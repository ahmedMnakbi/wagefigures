Most pay comparisons come down to one question: is this hourly rate better or worse than that salary? The arithmetic is simple, but a few details trip people up. This guide walks through the standard method, the exceptions, and the hidden factors that matter more than the headline number.

## The 2,080-hour rule

A full-time year is 40 hours a week for 52 weeks, which is 2,080 hours. To turn an hourly wage into an annual figure, multiply by 2,080:

- $15 an hour × 2,080 = $31,200 a year
- $22.50 an hour × 2,080 = $46,800 a year
- $35 an hour × 2,080 = $72,800 a year

To go the other way, divide the salary by 2,080. A $60,000 salary is $60,000 ÷ 2,080 = $28.85 an hour.

A quick mental shortcut: double the hourly rate and add three zeros. $20 an hour is roughly $40,000; the exact figure is $41,600. The shortcut undershoots by 4%, which is close enough for a first comparison.

## Monthly, biweekly and weekly

Once you have the yearly figure, the other periods follow:

| Period | Divide yearly by | $41,600 example |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly | 12 | $3,466.67 |
| Semi-monthly (24 paychecks) | 24 | $1,733.33 |
| Biweekly (26 paychecks) | 26 | $1,600.00 |
| Weekly | 52 | $800.00 |

Biweekly and semi-monthly are not the same. Biweekly pay arrives every other week, so two months a year contain three paychecks. Semi-monthly pay arrives twice a month on fixed dates. Over a year they total the same, but the biweekly check is about 8% smaller.

## When 2,080 is the wrong number

**Unpaid time off.** Hourly workers are usually paid only for hours worked. If you take two unpaid weeks, you work 50 weeks and earn 2,000 hours of pay. $20 an hour then yields $40,000, not $41,600. Salaried employees are normally paid through vacation and holidays, which is one reason a salary can be worth more than the equivalent hourly rate.

**Part-time schedules.** Replace 40 with your actual hours. At 30 hours a week, the multiplier is 30 × 52 = 1,560. $20 an hour at 30 hours is $31,200.

**Overtime.** Non-exempt hourly workers in the U.S. must be paid at least 1.5 times their regular rate for hours over 40 in a week. Five hours of overtime every week at $20 an hour adds $150 a week, or $7,800 a year. Overtime is not guaranteed, so treat it as upside, not baseline income.

**37.5-hour weeks.** Some employers define full time as 7.5 hours a day. The year is then 1,950 hours, and a $60,000 salary works out to $30.77 an hour instead of $28.85.

## Comparing an hourly job with a salaried one

Convert both to the same basis, then adjust for what the number leaves out:

1. **Paid time off.** Salaried roles often include two to four weeks of paid vacation plus holidays. For an hourly job, subtract the unpaid weeks you expect to take.
2. **Overtime eligibility.** Hourly workers get paid for every extra hour. Exempt salaried staff do not. If a salaried role routinely runs 50 hours, divide by 2,600 hours, not 2,080, to see the real hourly rate.
3. **Benefits.** Employer health insurance, retirement matching and paid leave can be worth 20–30% of base pay. A salary with benefits may beat a higher hourly rate without them.
4. **Stability.** Hourly schedules can be cut in slow periods. A salary is the same every month.

## Converting a raise

Raises are usually quoted as a percentage. A 4% raise on $52,000 is $2,080 a year, which is exactly $1 an hour more on a 2,080-hour year. That coincidence is handy: on a full-time schedule, every $2,080 of annual salary equals $1 an hour.

## Taxes

All of the figures above are gross pay. Federal income tax, Social Security and Medicare, state and sometimes local tax, and benefit deductions come out before you see the money. Take-home pay for most full-time workers lands somewhere between 70% and 85% of gross, depending on income, state and filing status. Use an official withholding estimator for your own paycheck; the ratio is different for everyone.

## Worked example

An offer of $26 an hour versus a salary of $55,000:

- $26 × 2,080 = $54,080. The salary is $920 higher on paper.
- The hourly role offers no paid vacation. Two unpaid weeks cut it to $52,000.
- The hourly role pays overtime; the salaried role expects occasional unpaid late nights.
- Both include similar health coverage.

On those facts the salary is worth about $3,000 more a year before any overtime. If you reliably work five overtime hours a week, the hourly job pulls ahead by roughly $7,000. The right answer depends on how much overtime is actually available.
